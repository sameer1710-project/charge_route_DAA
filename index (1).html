Step 1 · Understand it

The problem, in plain words

Before any code: what are we solving, why does it matter, and what will the program actually compute?

What is the problem?

You are driving an electric car on a long highway trip. The battery only covers a fixed range, and the charging stations along the road are at different distances and charge different prices. You must choose where to stop so the car never runs flat and the total charging cost is as low as possible.

Why it matters in real life

EV adoption is growing quickly, but charging networks are uneven: long gaps between chargers, fast-charger price premiums and constant range anxiety. A poor plan means being stranded or overpaying. Navigation apps, fleet operators and bus and cab companies solve exactly this kind of optimisation every day.

What the program computes

Input: trip distance, battery range, and a list of stations (position in km + charging cost).
Output: the set of stops that gets the car from start to destination with no gap longer than the range at the lowest total cost — computed four different ways so you can compare them.

Assumptions: the car leaves with a full battery · every stop recharges it to full · each station has one flat price per charge · n = the number of charging stations.
